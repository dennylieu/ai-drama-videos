# How we sell Mediskiin (Brazilified)

The master sales argument for MediSkiin NAD+ EGF Firming Serum. Every ad, landing page, end card and primary text
pulls from this. It follows the 4 sales (`.claude/skills/creative-strategy/brazilify.md`): we never open on the product.
We sell the story, then hope, then value, then urgency. Customer language comes from
`research/neck-reddit-voc-2026-11-13.md` (VOC #n).
`[BRACKETS]` = a fact only the brand can confirm. Fill these before launch; never invent them.

## The one-sentence version
**Her face still looks like her, but her neck gives her away. Everything she tried sat on top of the skin. Mediskiin is
the red Korean serum that sinks in (you can watch it disappear) and helps her neck look firmer and smoother in a few
weeks, with no needles and no surgery, at less than [PRICE/DAY] a day.**

## Who we're selling to
- **Market:** anti-aging, entered through the neck (turkey neck, crepey skin, neck bands).
- **Segments, in order of priority:**
  1. *Face-neck contrast:* "My face barely has any wrinkles (I'm 57), but my neck is really bad. The contrast looks weird." (VOC #2)
  2. *Betrayed or divorced woman:* proven in competitor drama hooks; she wants to look like herself again, on her terms.
  3. *Zoom woman:* "When I see myself on the webcam, all I see is that my neck is a mess." (VOC #13)
  4. *Surgery-reluctant:* "I will do anything short of surgery." (VOC #7)
- **The avatar:** a US woman, 45–65, who tilts her chin up in every photo, owns a shelf of neck creams that "make it soft but
  nothing tightens", has priced a neck lift and is scared of it. She watches courtroom and divorce TV, reality TV and
  true crime, trusts Korean skincare, and distrusts "miracle" claims.

## Sale 1: Content (make her watch)
We don't sell skincare in the first seconds. We sell a **story she can't scroll past**, in a format she already watches.

- **Format:** prestige TV drama (courtroom, divorce, betrayal). Looks like her shows, not an ad.
- **Topic:** age, and the people who judge it: an ex-husband, a court, a DMV clerk, a passport.
- **Concept:** a woman whose age is "on trial", and the proof is her neck.
  *Flagship:* "He married her in Vegas thinking she was 34. She's 61. Now he's suing for fraud."
- **Visual hook (frame 1):** the proof. A passport or ID with the birth year, held up next to her smooth neck.
- **Copy hook (first line):** the contrast. "He married her thinking she was 34. She's 61." / "POV: my husband sued me for looking too young."

**Rule:** the age-contrast proof lands in the **first 3 seconds** of every ad.

## Sale 2: Hope (make her believe it can work this time)
She's been burned. We don't argue with her; we **prove she was right** and show what was missing.

1. **Identification:** we show her exact moments: chin tilted up for the photo, a turtleneck in July, the noon Zoom, the
   shelf of jars. *"By the noon Zoom I'm sticking my chin out. By night it's all I can see."*
2. **Point of contact (what she already blames):** *"The creams just sit on top."* She already believes this (VOC #33–#36).
   We agree with her.
3. **Problem mechanism (why it never worked):** *"Your creams soften the surface, but the layer where new skin is made never
   gets the message, and looking down at your phone all day folds it deeper."* [Confirm the wording with the brand's
   science; keep it cosmetic.]
4. **Function (what has to happen, and why what she tried couldn't do it):** *"Something has to reach that deeper layer
   and wake renewal back up."* Retinol "works on the skin and stops there" and stings. Neck Botox "did absolutely
   nothing". Lights and devices are "pretty lights". Surgery cuts the skin away and leaves her sleeping upright for two weeks.
5. **Solution:** *EGF* is the "grow" signal; *NAD+* is the fuel. In a light serum that sinks in.
6. **Proof she can see:** *"It goes on red, and you watch the red disappear into your skin."* This is our demo: show it
   on camera, close up, every time.
7. **Conditions (why not just any EGF serum):** [Needs product truth. Candidate: EGF and NAD+ are delicate; most products
   put trace amounts in a heavy cream that sits on top; Mediskiin is a light Korean-formulated serum made to absorb, which
   is why the red vanishes.] Only use what the brand can back up.

## Sale 3: Value (make this the obvious choice, at this price)
- **USP:** the only place she gets both EGF and NAD+ in a serum she can *see* sink in, formulated by Korean skin experts.
- **Features, by lever:**
  - *Odds:* two actives working together (signal + fuel), not another single-ingredient cream.
  - *Dream made tangible:* open necklines again, photos without the chin trick, a Zoom with no wince, letting the kids take
    the picture. In the film: "No scarf. No high collar. Never a chin-up selfie. Every angle, every light."
  - *Effort:* a few drops, morning and night. No needles, no downtime, no appointments.
  - *Time:* "over a few weeks", with the reason: skin renews in cycles, so the change builds week by week. Never "overnight".
- **Anchoring, the cost of NOT fixing it:**
  - *Money:* "6 grand here, 2k on lasers there" (VOC #38); a neck lift costs thousands; a shelf of $50–$100 jars that did nothing.
  - *Emotion:* years of hiding in photos and avoiding mirrors.
  - *Time:* "It's just going to get worse." (VOC #47)
  - *The line:* "About [PRICE/DAY] a day. Less than one of the jars on your shelf, and a fraction of one laser session."

## Sale 4: Impulse (make now the safe choice)
- **Risk reversal:** "Try it for [GUARANTEE] days. That's longer than it takes to see the change in the mirror. If you don't,
  you get your money back." Frame it against the real timeline.
- **Gain now:** [OFFER, e.g. Buy 2 Get 2 Free + gifts], [free shipping], [ship speed]. Explain it bundle by bundle.
- **Lose by waiting:** [the deadline the price ends], [stock, if true]. Never fake scarcity.

## Words we use / avoid
- **Use:** turkey neck, crepey, neck bands, "sits on top", "sinks in", "looks firmer", "smoother", "the red disappears",
  "Korean skin experts", "no needles", "a few weeks".
- **Avoid:** "rebuilds collagen", "heals", "reverses aging", "replaces a neck lift", "clinically proven" (unless true and
  documented), before/after photos, "years younger" numbers. *Note: the label lists collagen extract, so never attack
  "collagen creams".*
