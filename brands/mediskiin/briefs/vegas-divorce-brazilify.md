## Step 2: Target

### Q1. Which markets can the product enter? (a market = people sharing one desire)

| Market | Evidence | Competitive heat |
|---|---|---|
| **Anti-aging face (wrinkles)** | The main competitor's ads are overwhelmingly face-wrinkle ads ("Say goodbye to wrinkles…") | 🔥 Proven, biggest |
| **Neck (turkey neck, crepey, tech neck)** | Our VOC (48 quotes); a competitor's live Ad Library ads "7 Reasons This Korean Neck Cream Is Selling Out" | 🔥 Proven, a competitor is moving into it |
| Loose skin after weight loss (GLP-1, "Ozempic neck") | VOC #8, #13, #30, #31, #43, #46, #48 | Trending, growing |
| Menopause skin | Competitor ads aimed at menopausal women | Likely proven |

**Choice (Derick: copy the market that's already working, save the risk for concept and format):**
anti-aging, entered through the **neck**. That's our VOC strength, and the competitor is moving there too. Keep "face"
as a separate test, because that's where most competitor ads sit.

### Q2. Which segment? (a cluster sharing a condition, identity or behaviour)
Competitor drama hooks like "I married a cheater" and "POV: I married a narcissist" show segments by **life
situation** (betrayed, divorced, menopausal), not by age. The rule: copy the segments that work
for a competitor (way #1); find new ones on Reddit (way #2).

| Segment | Way | Fit with the Vegas ad |
|---|---|---|
| **Face looks young, neck gives her away** (VOC #2, #8, #11) | #2, our Reddit | ✅ The whole film: "you woke up next to that face every morning" |
| **Betrayed / divorced woman** | #1, copy the competitor | ✅ Vivian is the victim of a fortune-hunting husband |
| Webcam / Zoom woman (VOC #13, #14) | #2 | ✅ Denise: "by the noon Zoom I'm sticking my chin out" |
| Lost weight, skin stayed (VOC #13, #30, #31) | #2 | ❌ not in this film; a separate concept |
| Surgery-reluctant (VOC #7, #33, #40) | #2 | ✅ Denise: "You think I had a lift or filler? I didn't" |

**Choice for the Vegas fix:** betrayed woman (copied) + face-neck contrast (ours). Two proven layers; the bet is the concept.

### Q3. Who is the avatar?
**Layer 1: Psychographics (stolen from the research and the winners, not reinvented)**
- **Primary desire:** a neck that matches her face; to stop looking older than she feels.
- **Problem:** loose, crepey, banded neck skin ("turkey neck", "gobbler"); creams soften but don't firm.
- **Secondary desires:** photos without posing; open necklines in summer; a Zoom without wincing; looking good to a partner or ex.
- **Pain points (real moments):** profile photo shock (VOC #11), webcam (#13), chin out in every photo (#16), turtlenecks in summer (#22, #23), a shelf of failed creams (#25–#29).
- **Dreams:** "having a smooth neck is such a dream" (#19); letting her kids take photos (#42).
- **Fears:** "it's just going to get worse" (#47); surgery's pain and cost (#40); turning into her mother's "full-on waddle" (#6).

**Layer 2: Identity**
- **Demographics:** US women 45–65; married, divorced or remarried; often the family's photo-taker who avoids being in photos.
- **Behaviour (where hooks come from):** watches TV courtroom and divorce shows, reality TV, true crime, Lifetime and Hallmark
  dramas; heavy Facebook and Reels use; follows celebrity age-gap couples; hears constantly about Ozempic and "Botox in a
  bottle"; works on Zoom; curious about Korean skincare ("Korean women don't age").
  *To validate: pull search and trend data before betting a concept on any of these.*
- **Perspective:**
  - Beliefs: "creams can't penetrate the deeper layers" (#33), "collagen is too big a molecule" (#34), "surgery is the only real fix" (#36, #39).
  - Biases: distrusts "magic" claims (#41), trusts Korean beauty.
  - Enemies: overpriced creams ("pretty lights", #37), upselling derms, "just accept aging".
  - Allies: Korean skin experts, other women who "figured it out".
  - Language: turkey neck, gobbler, crepey, bands, "the contrast", "all I can see".

## Step 3: Brief

### A. Mechanism (only what the avatar is already predisposed to accept)

**1) Point of contact: what she already blames**
She already believes the creams sit on top and can't reach the deeper layers (VOC #33–#36). The ad already says
"Everything else sat on top" (04:59, 07:48). ✅ Keep it: we connect dots, we don't argue.

**2) Problem mechanism: the missing reason that proves her suspicion right**
Proposed, in cosmetic language: *"Neck skin renews more slowly as we age, and it gets folded all day looking down at
phones (VOC #18, #21). Creams soften the surface, but the layer where new skin is made never gets the signal."*
In the ad now: "Stalled cell clearance… dull dead cells" (04:28), which is vague. ⚠️ Needs product truth from the brand
before stating any biology as fact.

**3) Function mechanism: what has to happen, and why everything she tried can't do it**
*"Something has to reach the living layer and switch renewal back on."* Why the alternatives failed, in her words:
- retinol "works on our skin, and stops there" (#35) and irritates;
- neck Botox "did absolutely nothing" (#32);
- devices are "pretty lights" (#37);
- surgery cuts the skin off instead of renewing it, with "sleep sitting up… compression garment 23 hours a day" (#40).

In the ad now: "I've tried neck creams / expensive neck creams / retinol A, too much" (04:09). ✅ Good; add a Botox and
"lift or filler" contrast (already partly at 03:56).

**4) Solution mechanism: the one thing that performs the function**
EGF = the "grow" signal; NAD+ = the "fuel"; the serum format sinks in. Built-in **proof device:** the red serum
visibly disappears into the skin (05:01 "This just disappeared into it"). ✅ This is the demo. Show it longer and closer.

### B. Content

**1) Topic: a subject she already cares about (from behaviour)**
Candidates: a Vegas wedding annulment over age (current); age-gap couples; a passport or ID showing she's older than she
looks (the competitor's DMV winner); "Ozempic neck"; Zoom face. **The current topic is right.** It's courtroom TV plus
an age reveal, the same family as the competitor's ID-check winner.

**2) Format**
- a) **Look:** prestige courtroom drama with a glossy Vegas flashback. ✅ Matches her shows.
- b) **Narrative style:** serialized twists (annulment → countersuit → murder plot → FBI). ✅ Holds attention; the risk is that the product feels like a detour.
- c) **Characters and roles:**
  - Vivian (61, looks 34): the *aspiration*, the proof the product works.
  - Denise (grey turtleneck, hides her neck): the *avatar's mirror*, she asks the avatar's questions.
  - Tyler and Brianna: the villains, the "enemy".
  - Marcus: the authority who states the proof aloud.

**3) Concept**
- a) **Logline:** "A young husband sues to annul his Vegas marriage for fraud because his 61-year-old wife looks 34. Then
  her lawyer proves he married her to kill her."
- b) **The angle that makes her need to hear more:** *"He sued her for looking too young."* Today the first 30 s play as
  a money dispute ("half of everything, two hotels, a jet"), and the age-contrast angle isn't stated until 01:54 or 02:56.

**4) Copy hook**
Derick picks a proven hook *structure* first, then fills it in. The proven structures for this market:
- "POV: …" (the competitor's newer winners)
- "He married her thinking she was 34…" (contrast statement)
- "This is what happens when…"

| Option | Hook |
|---|---|
| A (contrast, recommended) | "He married her thinking she was 34. She's 61." |
| B (POV) | "POV: My husband sued me for looking too young." |
| C (evidence) | "Exhibit 15: born June 2nd, 1965. Look at her neck." |

Current hook: "34! You said 34!", which only makes sense once you know the story.

**5) Visual hook**
A proven competitor winner opens on an official holding an ID and doubting the age on it. **We already shot our
version:** Marcus holding the passport, "Exhibit 15, born June 2nd, 1965" (02:54), followed by "Look at her neck, there's
not a single line on it" (07:08). Open on those frames, ideally with a tight shot of her neck.

### C. Offer (Value Sale)

**1) Conditions: why she can't get this herself**
The ad is missing this today. The objection it must answer: "Lots of serums have EGF." Draft, true ONLY if the brand
confirms it: *"EGF and NAD+ are delicate. Most creams use tiny amounts in a thick base that sits on top. It has to be a
light serum, formulated to sink in, which is why the red disappears instead of staying on your skin."*
→ **Needs product truth (formulation, concentration, Korean lab) from the brand.**

**2) USP: why Mediskiin is the place to get it under those conditions**
Map each condition to a feature: the light red serum (sinks in, visibly), EGF + NAD+ together (signal + fuel),
formulated by Korean skin experts. Don't invent differentiators.

**3) Features, filtered by the 4 levers (anything that doesn't serve one is cut)**
- **Odds:** two actives working together (signal + fuel) vs. single-active creams.
- **Dream made tangible:** open necklines, photos without the chin trick, a Zoom without wincing. *In the film:* Vivian's
  "no scarf, no high collar, never a chin-up selfie" (02:44) is exactly this; reuse the line in the copy.
- **Effort:** a few drops, morning and night, no needles, no downtime.
- **Time:** "over a few weeks" (copy only). Add *why* it takes weeks (skin renews in cycles), so the timeline feels
  believable rather than hyped.

**4) Anchoring: why not fixing it costs more than the price**
- **Money:** "6 grand on teeth and rubber bands… 2k on lasers" (VOC #38); a neck lift "thousands" (#33); a shelf of $50–$100 creams.
- **Emotion:** hiding in photos for years; "evidence of my trauma every time I look in the mirror" (#20).
- **Time:** "it's just going to get worse" (#47).
- **The line:** "About [PRICE/DAY] a day. Less than one of the creams on your shelf, and a fraction of a single laser session."

The film's "jars that cost more than a weekend at the Wynn" (03:37) is a great emotional anchor; the price contrast is missing.

### D. Close (Impulse Sale)
1. **Risk reversal:** "[GUARANTEE] days to see it in the mirror. That's longer than it takes to notice the difference." Frame it against the real timeline, like Derick's 120 days vs 10 weeks. **UNKNOWN guarantee.**
2. **Gain now:** "Buy 2, get 2 free, plus gifts" (if still live), free shipping, ship speed.
3. **Lose by waiting:** the Black Friday price ends [DATE]; stock, only if true.
