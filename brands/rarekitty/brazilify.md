## THE MECHANISM (named) + THE ANALOGY

**Problem mechanism (villain): "The Paint Creep."**
Regular lipstick is a *film*: wax, oil and pigment painted **on top** of your lips. As lips change, the film doesn't move with them. It dries and cracks on the surface, and the oily color creeps into every fine line around your mouth.

**Solution mechanism (hero): "Dual-Reactive pH + Thermochromic Color Shift."**
- **Dual-reactive, step 1:** it goes on **clear**, a balm with nothing to see and nothing to "get right."
- **Step 2:** it **reacts** with the pH and warmth of *your* lips and develops into *your* rose.

In plain words: **it stains, it doesn't paint.**

*(Every word is literally true: the color is pH- and temperature-reactive (confirmed by the owner), it reacts to 2 things (pH and temperature), goes on clear and shifts color on the lips. Use the correct spelling "thermochromic," never "thermochromatic." Don't add "deep," "cellular" or "clinical." They wouldn't be true.)*

**The one analogy: painting vs staining wood** (her world: the porch, the deck, the kitchen table)

| Mechanism | Wood |
|---|---|
| Regular lipstick (film on top) | **Paint** on an old porch: sits on top |
| Cracking / flaking / cakey | Paint that **cracks and peels** |
| Creeping into lip lines | Paint that **pools in the grooves** of the wood |
| Picking a shade that's wrong on you | Painting over the wood until you can't see it |
| The color shifting lipstick goes on clear | Clear **stain** going on |
| Develops into your own rose | The stain **brings out the wood's own color** |
| Result | "It looks like *you*, only richer." |

**Callback plan (5 touches):**
1. Function mechanism: "Every lipstick in your drawer is paint."

3. Price/anchor: "Five tubes of stain for less than you've spent on paint you never wear."
