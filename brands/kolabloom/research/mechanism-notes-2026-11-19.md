## The brand's approved mechanism ("How it all connects", Nov 2 master research)
1. Your legs are your "second heart": the calf muscles pump blood back up to the heart.
2. That pump runs on collagen: in the vessel walls (the pipes) and in the fascia around the calf (the sponge).
3. After 50 that collagen breaks down: the pipes leak, so fluid pools in the ankles; the sponge dries out, so the
   calves go stiff and squeeze weakly. Blood pools below and the real heart works harder.
4. Gotu kola's four triterpenes signal fibroblasts to build fresh Type I collagen and slow the enzymes that break it
   down: the pipes firm up, the sponge holds water again, the second heart pumps.
Brand metaphors: calves = a toothpaste tube; valves = one-way doors; vessel walls = a garden hose (old ones get saggy
and leaky); fascia = a kitchen sponge left on the windowsill; fibroblasts = little construction workers.
Brand's comparison: water pills force water out through the kidneys and leave leaky capillaries; compression squeezes
from outside and leaves weak walls; Kolabloom works on the barrier and wall collagen. "Not a diuretic."

## UMP / UMS (brand's own)
- Problem: "mistaking ingredient count for meaningful exposure": 10-20 herb blends at 10-50 mg each.
- Solution: one botanical, a disclosed 1,000 mg, a simple routine: a **formulation differentiator, not a newly
  discovered pathway**.

## Evidence for legs (human)
- Pointel 1987: 94 patients, TECA 60-120 mg/day, 60 days: less heaviness, ankle circumference down vs placebo.
- De Sanctis 2001: TTFCA 90-180 mg/day, 4 weeks: capillary filtration and ankle fluid down.
- Cesarone 2001 flight trial: TTFCA around long-haul flights (>10 h): less ankle swelling. (Travel: fits a cruise.)
- Chong & Aziz 2013: review of 8 trials: better microcirculation, less edema; quality limited.

## Limits the brand's own research records (we must respect these)
- The leg trials used **concentrated triterpene fractions at 60-180 mg**, not 1,000 mg of whole leaf. The 1,000 mg
  precedents are cognition studies. The brief says: "These studies do not establish Kolabloom plaque/circulation
  benefits" and "a studied dose is not a minimum effective dose or proof of superiority".
  -> **Do not say "the dose the research used" for legs** (the cruise native does; the brand's own file contradicts it).
- Don't call competitors underdosed without a label audit; "higher mg" ≠ more active compound.
- Oral capsules opening lymph vessels, draining the face or "cleaning the blood": not established.
- No NO claims from the CVI trials; cognition, metabolic, kidney and anti-ageing findings are preclinical or limited:
  out of scope for this ad.
