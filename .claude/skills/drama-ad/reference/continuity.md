# Bridges and continuity (why drama ads lose viewers mid-way)

Adapted from Idan Frenkel's breakdown of a failed drama ad (X, Nov 2026). A strong hook is wasted if the middle
confuses people: **the second a viewer has to ask where they are or who someone is, they swipe.**

Check every script and every edit against these before generating:

1. **Bridge every scene change.** Show how we got there: walking to the car, entering the café, ordering, spotting the
   friend, or a "That morning" card. Never drop the viewer into a new place mid-conversation.
2. **No unexplained people.** Anyone who talks has been seen or set up before (a glance, a seat in the gallery, an
   introduction). Random bystanders commenting = confusion.
3. **No oracles.** An answer must follow a question asked on screen ("Then why didn't mine work?"). A character announcing
   "it was never my fault" when nobody asked breaks belief.
4. **Believable timelines.** Use 1, 2, 3, 4 or 6 weeks, not "one year later". Every timeframe in the ad agrees: the
   result's timeline, any "thank me in N days" and the guarantee (e.g. 4 weeks to see it, 90 days to return).
5. **Props that make sense where they are.** The product appears where a real person would have it (a handbag at a
   sink, a bathroom shelf), never at a party with no reason.
6. **When cutting down existing footage, keep the bridging lines and shots** ("Inside", "Recess", "Back in session");
   trimming them creates the same jumps.

QC step: watch the cut muted at 2× and, at every cut, answer "where are we, who is this, why are they here?" If any
answer is missing, add a bridge before shipping.
