Patterns to copy:

- Open on an object insert, not a face. The first line begins over the insert (sound leads picture).
- Fixed setups. Every return to a character is the same framing. Never a third angle on them.
- Ping-pong cuts of 1.2 to 1.8s, then one long hold, then at most one wide shot, used last.
- One silence (0.8s after "I am sixty-three"), placed where the attitude flips. Always leave one.
- The curiosity line lands at about 60 percent and is never explained in the opening segment.

### Look, as measured

- Camera: eye level, locked off, tiny natural drift only. Long-lens feel, shallow depth of field. No push-ins, no handheld shake, no whip pans.
- Framing: officer sits left of centre and looks frame right. Passenger sits centre and looks frame left, with the officer's dark shoulder soft in the bottom left corner. The eyeline never flips.
- Light: big windows behind and beside the actors. Soft daylight wrap, no hard key, no rim glow, no flare. Windows bright but not blown.
- Exposure: mean luma 76 to 96 out of 255, black floor about 8, highlights topping out at 176 to 224. Slightly under-exposed, lifted blacks, gentle highlight roll-off.
- Colour: warm neutral balance, muted saturation. Deep navy, cream, warm tan and gold only. Background is grey, glass and pale wood.
- Texture: soft, not sharp. Real skin with lines, pores and neck creases. Hair with flyaways. This softness is a large part of why it reads as film.
- Background: blurred travellers walking, a blurred hanging sign. Alive, never readable.

### Known flaws across the three films (prevent these)

- Printed documents render as gibberish (Cedar passport, a smudge of text on the "He does" watch face). Keep inserts of anything with text under 1.3s, angle the text away, or say "plain dial, no lettering".
- Uniforms pick up wrong insignia (pilot wings on the passport officer). Name the job and say what the uniform does not have.
- Props swap hands or change (the supervisor held a phone, not the passport). Say which hand holds which prop.
- Wardrobe bleeds between similar characters (one husband inherited the other's blazer in "He does").
- People described as sitting together get framed alone ("He does"). If the seating matters, ask for the neighbour's shoulder soft in each close-up.

## Netflix film grammar

The target is a scene from a feature, not an ad that looks expensive. Apply all of this on every film.

**Story**
- Write a scene, not a pitch. Each character wants something. The product answers a question the scene raises.
- One reversal only, with a silence before it.
- Subtext over statement. Nobody explains how they feel. No brand name spoken in the opening segment.
- Start late, leave early. End on an unanswered question or a look.

**Performance**
- Underplay. Direct with verbs the model can act: glances down, exhales through the nose, half smile that stops, eyebrows lift a little, holds eye contact a beat too long.
- Ask for a reaction (a blink, a swallow, eyes moving) before each reply.
- For the key emotional beat, name the muscles. "Lip slightly curled in mild disgust" rendered as a blank face. "Her nose wrinkles and her upper lip curls in clear open disgust, a tiny shake of the head" rendered correctly.
- Voices are dry, close, conversational. No announcer tone.

**Camera**
- Large-format digital cinema camera feel, about 50mm for inserts and 85mm for close-ups, wide open. Tripod, never handheld shake, never a zoom.
- Eye level for every lead. Close-ups slightly off-centre with looking room.
- If there is a wide shot, compose it like a master with three layers of depth.

**Light and grade**
- Motivated light only: windows, a bedside lamp, stage glow, a phone. No beauty light, no rim glow, no flare.
- Film-print grade: low contrast, lifted blacks, highlights that roll off, warm true skin against a cooler room.
- One three-colour palette per film, set in wardrobe and set, not in the grade.

**Production design**
- Lived-in and specific: a cluttered dresser, a scuffed counter, extras who are going somewhere. Nothing brand new.
- Wardrobe looks owned.

**Sound**
- Location-sound feel: close dialogue with the room's echo, plus small real sounds (paper, fabric, a clock, a seat creak).
- No score under dialogue. Sound leads picture on at least one cut.

**Edit**
- Cut on the breath or on a look. Hold one reaction longer than is comfortable. End on that hold.
