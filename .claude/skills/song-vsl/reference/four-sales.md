# The brief layer: 4 sales, 17 elements (Derick Carneiro)

Source: Derick Carneiro, "how I write ads that make strangers buy", X article, 2026-11-19
(https://x.com/derickcarneiro_/status/2123414633935520052). Brazilian VSL/ad method; worked example is a capsaicin
supplement (Aurivita). Summarised and adapted for this repo. What we left out and why is at the bottom.

## The idea
A cold-traffic ad doesn't sell the product. It makes **four sales before the product**, each with reasons the avatar
already wants to hear. Persuading = presenting an idea and giving reasons why it's true. Your job is not to say what
you think needs saying; it is to find **what** the avatar wants to hear, **how** they want to hear it, and **who**
they want to hear it from, and put it in one place.

## The 4 sales and 17 elements (and where each sits in a song VSL)
| Sale | # | Element | The reason it gives | Song VSL beat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Content** (watch to the end) | 1 | Format | Guard down, doesn't feel like an ad | It's a song and a drama |
| | 2 | Topic | Why they'd care about this subject | The fear + the situation |
| | 3 | Concept | Why they can't leave before the end | The hook's loop (logline + angle) |
| | 4 | Visual hook | Snaps them out of autopilot | Opening frame of S01 |
| | 5 | Copy hook | Decides whether they keep watching | First sung line |
| **Hope** (it can be solved) | 6 | Identification | "They get me" | Avatar + problem beats |
| | 7 | Point of contact | What they already suspect causes it | "It's stress / my age / my hormones" |
| | 8 | Problem mechanism | The missing reason that proves the suspicion right | Messenger reveals the hidden cause |
| | 9 | Function mechanism | The action needed, and why other solutions can't do it | Why everything she tried failed |
| | 10 | Solution mechanism | The one thing that does that action, with reasons | The ingredient and its job |
| | 11 | Conditions | Why she can't get it elsewhere / on her own | "Why not just eat/buy X?" answered |
| **Value** (this product) | 12 | USP | Why this product is the place to get it under those conditions | Product beat |
| | 13 | Features | Only those that raise odds, make the dream tangible, cut effort or explain the timeline | Product + proof |
| | 14 | Anchoring | Not solving it costs more (money, emotion, time) than the price | Close |
| **Impulse** (now) | 15 | Risk reversal | Not buying is riskier than buying | Close: the guarantee |
| | 16 | Gain by buying now | What extra they take home now | Close: bundles |
| | 17 | Lose by waiting | What they lose by putting it off (only if true) | Close: deadline |

## The process: premises -> target -> brief -> writing
**1. Premises** (what we already know; usually the product page): brand, product, ingredients and doses, attributes
(testing, format, daily routine, how long until results, side effects and warnings the page states), prices and
bundles, guarantee (and refund rate if the brand publishes it), what they gain now, what they lose by waiting.

**2. Target: market -> segment -> avatar.**
- *Market* = people sharing one desire. A product can serve several; pick ONE. Existing brand: the market its
  highest-traffic landers and most-run ads target. New product: the market with 3+ competitors and the most traffic
  growth in the last 30 days.
- *Segment* = a cluster in that market sharing a condition, identity or behaviour (postpartum women losing weight;
  diabetic men with the same problem). Copy the segment winners already target, or find an unserved one on Reddit.
- *Avatar*, two layers. Psychographics: primary desire, problem, secondary desires, pain points, dreams, fears.
  Identity: demographics (sex, age, nationality, language, ethnicity, religion, marital status, family role),
  behaviour (competitor ads and videos they watch, trends, hobbies, news, shows, other health conditions),
  perspective (beliefs, biases, enemies, allies, language).
- **Research where the leverage is.** Psychographics barely change over years and the winners already carry them:
  mine winning ads, advertorials and old VSLs in the same market and segment first. Spend your own hours on
  *identity/behaviour*: the news, videos, trends and searches ("can you mix X with Y?") that hold the avatar's real
  attention now. That's where topics, formats, concepts and hooks come from.

**3. Brief** (fill all 17 elements; template in `templates.md`).
- **Mechanism in three parts**, picking the version the avatar is most predisposed to accept, connecting dots they
  already have rather than teaching something new:
  point of contact (their suspicion) -> problem mechanism (the hidden reason that proves it right) -> function
  mechanism (the action that fixes it, and why what they tried, e.g. the famous pill, can't do it *for someone like
  them*) -> solution mechanism (the ingredient that does that action).
- **Conditions -> USP.** Answer "why can't I just get this myself?" (eat the food, buy the cheap capsule) with the
  conditions the ingredient needs to work (dose, form, absorption, tolerance). Then the USP is just the product
  meeting those conditions one by one, from the product page. No invented differentiator.
- **Features through 4 levers only:** odds (several angles of attack = more ways to work), dream scenario (what life
  feels like, not the ingredient), effort (one a day, no new diet, no stopping what they already take), time (why it
  takes weeks, in a way that makes sense: years of damage need weeks to turn around). A feature that answers none
  doesn't go in. Nobody buys "hawthorn"; they buy what it does for them.
- **Anchoring:** price per day vs the cost of the problem: money (every product that didn't work), emotion (the
  partner, the shame), time (the longer it waits, the harder it gets).
- **Risk reversal with a reason:** not "120-day guarantee" (everyone has one) but "that's almost twice the time
  your body needs to show results; the only risk is doing the same thing and expecting a different result".
- **Gain now / lose by waiting:** spell out bundle extras, shipping, a real deadline or real stock.
- **Content:** topic (a fact, news story or question the avatar cares about), format (look + narrative, reusing
  what's validated in this market), characters and roles, concept = characters + narrative + topic as a logline,
  then the angle that makes it irresistible. Copy hook: pick a proven hook structure from winners first ("This is
  what happens to ... when you ..."), then fill it in the avatar's slang. Visual hook: fits the format and concept
  and still breaks expectation.

**4. Writing.** Transcribe a proven reference ad and split it into *mini-blocks* (hook, belief break, mechanism
reveal, product presentation...). The **brief decides what gets said; the reference decides the order and rhythm.**
Writing is filling the blocks. AI that skips the brief guesses the psychology and writes bad ads.

## Bets and risk
Minimise risk where leverage is low (market, segment, mechanism: reuse what already works); raise the bet where
leverage is high (concept, format, hook). An ad's risk = how many new bets it carries. With a scaling brand, run a
few conservative ads, many moderate, some aggressive. A winning bet lifts scale and pays for the misses.
For us: a song VSL is itself a format bet. Keep market, segment and mechanism proven; bet on the story and the song.

## What we left out (and why)
- **Copying a real game's look and character** (the example used a GTA VI protagonist). That's someone else's IP and
  fails our rules and Seedance's `ip_detected`. We bet on original looks only.
- **Sexualised styling of the presenter.** Not our format; trips platform review and `nsfw`.
- **Medical/sexual-performance claims and drug-interaction fear hooks** ("don't mix X with your prescription").
  Health claims must stay inside the brand's claims list and platform rules; never tell viewers anything about their
  medication.
- **Scarcity or refund-rate numbers the brand can't prove.** Deadlines, stock and stats only when true.
