# The Ryze song-VSL blueprint (full breakdown)

Source: Ori Silver, "Inside Ryze's $22M Song Ads: The Blueprint Behind Their Winning VSLs", X article,
2026-09-25 (https://x.com/OriSilver/status/2103566774687969435). Summarised and rewritten for this repo; Ryze
sells powdered wellness drink blends and its best ads are 7-10 minute songs, reported at over $22M revenue a month.

## Everything starts with the sub-avatar
The hero's age, the betrayal, the setting, the mentor and the pain point are all picked to fit one specific person
and the fear they keep to themselves.

Example: a Black American man, 47, married, carrying thirty extra pounds, a soft chest and a lost jawline, quietly
afraid his wife no longer finds him attractive.

When the sub-avatar changes, every beat changes with it. That is why Ryze runs several versions of the same ad.

## The hero's journey (a fall and a rise)

| # | Beat | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ordinary world | A few seconds of the long marriage, family, routine: just enough to show what will be lost. | Twenty years married, Sunday cookouts. |
| 2 | Humiliation (the hook) | The ad opens on it. The first line drops the hero into something interesting and bad. | Wife cheating with his best man. Husband cheating with her sister. Wife thinks he doesn't want her anymore. |
| 3 | The wound deepens | Worse, right away, unexpected. This is where the target viewer is hooked. | The whole friend group knew for two years. He overhears her talking about him. |
| 4 | Frozen | The hero doesn't fight. Freezing is relatable and keeps the pain in; anger would drain the tension. | Smiles through the party for four more hours. Pretends to be asleep. |
| 5 | Seeing it too | The hero looks at their own body and agrees with the insult. | Counts the thirty pounds, the soft chest, the gray in his beard. |
| 6 | Failed solutions | Takes apart what the viewer believes works. Some advice comes from friends; a dismisser calls it normal. Rock bottom. | Gym, cutting beer, 75-day challenge, testosterone pills, doctor says it's just age. |
| 7 | The mentor | Someone close (family, college roommate) who went through the same thing, visibly credible, the finished version of the hero. | Brother who runs a lymphatic clinic for an Olympic team. |
| 8 | The gift | The mentor reveals the real cause; what the hero believed falls apart. Then hands over the product. | |
| 9 | Doubt | The hero doesn't believe it; they've tried everything. Voices the viewer's doubt so the viewer converts with them. | She refuses the pills her betraying sister took, until her mother says take them anyway. |
| 10 | The climb | Small, concrete, week-by-week changes that other people notice; each answers a piece of the insult. | Week two, a coworker asks what changed. |
| 11 | Vindication | Same place, same people, transformed; the exact reversal of the opening insult. | See shapes below. |
| 12 | Return with the elixir | The hero turns to the viewer and hands over the solution. The pitch, about the last minute. | |

**Vindication shapes**
- Male revenge: the man whose wife left him for his best man walks into the next cookout; she asks what he did.
- Female revenge: the woman whose husband cheated with her sister shows up at the school play; her ex's face drops;
  she leaves his messages on read.
- Reconciliation: the husband who kept saying he was too tired tells his wife he isn't; she admits she thought he
  didn't want her anymore.

## The characters, and why each one is chosen
The betrayal always comes from the closest person possible. When someone that close breaks the hero, the hero was
living a lie, and the viewer takes that home: "I have this too, so this could be happening to me."

| Role | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | The viewer's mirror | Andre, 47 |
| Partner | Sets the stakes: love or humiliation | The wife who says he looks sixty |
| Betrayer | Closest person possible, deepest wound | The best man and godfather |
| Witnesses | Watch the humiliation and do nothing; later watch the vindication | Friends at the cookout |
| Dismisser | The false solution the viewer already tried | Doctor who calls it aging |
| Mentor | The trusted, complete version of the hero | Brother who runs an Olympic clinic |
| Converts | Show that it spreads | Cousin who orders on the spot |

The pain is always dialogue from other characters; the hero never describes themselves that way.

## Emotional stakes beat explanation
The ad sells the emotional experience, not the mechanism. The body symptom matters only for what it costs in love
and respect: the hero wants their partner to look at them again; the smaller waist is how they get there.
Emotional stakes drive retention, and they filter: whoever is still watching deep into the video is the target and
converts at a very high rate.

## The mentor always comes before the product
The solution must come from someone the hero trusts: brother, sister, mother, college roommate. Never a casual friend,
never a salesperson. Credible (e.g. twenty years working with athletes, or obviously successful) and the complete
version of who the hero could become, and the product is what got them there.

## The build to vindication
Results come week by week, small and concrete, noticed by others. The climb ends where the ad started, with the dream
image the hero wanted from the beginning: the exact reversal of the opening insult. The pitch comes right after, at
the emotional peak, short (about the last minute) because the story already sold.

## Adapting it here (our notes, not from the article)
- "Credible label" for the mentor: we show it through life and setting (the clinic, the athletes, the fit body), not
  a fake real professional or medical claims. Platform rules on health claims and AI people still apply.
- Ryze's examples involve weight, testosterone and "lymphatic" claims. Our lyrics keep claims to what another person
  could see and to the brand's approved claims list.
