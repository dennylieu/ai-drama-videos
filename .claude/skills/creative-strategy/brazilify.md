# "Brazilify": the 4-sales, 17-element ad psychology (full process)

Adapted (own wording) from Derick Carneiro's article "how I write ads that make strangers buy" (X, Nov 2026).
Use it to brief new cold-traffic ads and to audit existing ones (drama, UGC, VSL, static). Worked example:
`brands/mediskiin/briefs/vegas-divorce-brazilify.md`.

## Core idea
Cold traffic knows its problem, has tried things, and is skeptical. Opening on product, price or offer fails at scale.
A cold ad makes **4 sales before the product**, each made with *reasons the avatar already wants to hear*, said the way
they want to hear them, by someone they want to hear them from. Persuasion = an idea plus reasons it's true.
The skill is not inventing clever arguments; it's **selecting** the reasons with the highest odds.

## The 17 elements, with the question each one answers

### Sale 1: Content ("this is worth watching to the end")
1. **Format:** why they lower their guard and don't suspect a sale. *Does it look like what they already watch (a show, news, a clip, a podcast)?*
2. **Topic:** why they care. *What fact, subject or situation already has their attention?*
3. **Concept:** why they can't leave. *Characters + narrative + topic in one logline, plus the angle that creates an "uncontrollable urge" to hear more.*
4. **Visual hook:** what breaks autopilot. *What in frame 1 fits the format, matches the concept, and still breaks expectations?*
5. **Copy hook:** the first words that decide whether they stay. *Pick a proven hook STRUCTURE first, then fill it with the concept. Choose the emotional lever (pain, fear, desire, curiosity).*

### Sale 2: Hope ("it failed before for a reason; this time it can work")
6. **Identification:** they feel understood, using their exact moments and language.
7. **Point of contact:** what they already suspect is the cause. *Use what most ads in the segment already blame; don't fight their belief.*
8. **Mechanism of the problem:** the missing reason that proves the suspicion right. *Connect dots they already half-know.*
9. **Mechanism of the function:** the action needed, and why what they tried can't do it. *Use their own failed solutions and fears (e.g. "the little blue pill wasn't made for diabetics").*
10. **Mechanism of the solution:** the one thing that performs the action, with reasons.
11. **Conditions:** why they can't just get it themselves (the pre-empted "I'll buy cayenne at the store"). *What form, dose or delivery makes it work, and why that's rare?*

### Sale 3: Value ("this product, at this price, is the only sensible way")
12. **USP:** the product delivers each Condition, one by one. *Don't invent differentiators; map the features to the conditions.*
13. **Features:** list only those that pull one of 4 levers: **odds** (why it works this time), **dream made tangible**, **effort minimized**, **time** (a timeline that makes sense, with the reason it takes that long). Nobody buys an ingredient; they buy what it does.
14. **Anchoring:** not solving it costs more in money, emotion and time than the price. *Price per day vs. the cost of living with the problem or of the alternatives.*

### Sale 4: Impulse ("buying now is the safe decision")
15. **Risk reversal:** not buying is riskier than buying. *Frame the guarantee against the real result timeline ("120 days, nearly double the 10 weeks you need").*
16. **Gain by buying now:** bundle by bundle, what extra they take home (free units, shipping, speed, gifts).
17. **Lose by waiting:** the deadline, stock, restock wait, price going back up. *Only true ones.*

## Process

### Step 1: Premises
Brand · product · ingredients · attributes (testing, dose, how to use, results timeline, side notes) · price ladder ·
guarantee (and refund rate if known) · gain now · lose later. Usually all on the product page. Mark unknowns; never invent them.

### Step 2: Target
- **Q1 Market** (people sharing one desire): list the markets the product or category can serve. For a running brand,
  pick where its highest-traffic landing pages and most recent ads (last 60 days) concentrate. For a new product, need
  3+ competitors per market and pick the one with the most traffic growth over the last 30 days. Don't gamble on new markets.
- **Q2 Segment** (a cluster sharing a condition, identity or behaviour): way 1, copy the segments working for you or
  competitors (low risk); way 2, find unclaimed segments on Reddit.
- **Q3 Avatar:**
  - *Psychographics:* primary desire, problem, secondary desires, pain points, dreams, fears.
    **Steal them** from winners (yours, competitors', old VSLs, advertorials); they barely change over the years.
  - *Identity:* demographics (sex, age, nationality, language, ethnicity, religion, marital status, family role);
    **behaviour** (competitor ads they click, videos they watch, trends, hobbies, news, shows, related conditions);
    perspective (beliefs, biases, enemies, allies, language).
    **Spend the research time on behaviour:** that's where topics, formats, concepts and hooks come from.

### Step 3: Brief (fill all 17)
1. **Mechanism:** point of contact → problem → function → solution, each the version the avatar is most predisposed to accept (evidence: what every ad in the segment says, search queries, news).
2. **Content:** topic (list many, pick one) → format (a. look, b. narrative style, c. characters and roles; borrow pieces from 2+ working ads) → concept (logline + angle) → copy hook (proven structure + concept) → visual hook (proven visual + a twist that raises the shock).
3. **Offer:** conditions → USP → features (4 levers) → anchoring.
4. **Close:** risk reversal → gain → lose.

### Step 4: Write
Split a proven reference ad into **mini-blocks** (hook, belief break, mechanism reveal, product, offer, close) and pour
the brief into them, by hand or with AI given BOTH the brief and the reference. The brief decides WHAT is said; the
reference decides ORDER and RHYTHM. AI without the brief = generic copy.

## Risk management
- Minimize risk where leverage is low (market, segment, mechanism: reuse what works).
- Raise the bet where leverage is high (concept, format, hook: e.g. a new visual style riding a trend).
- Risk = the number of new bets in one ad. Each batch: a few conservative, many moderate, some aggressive.
  One aggressive hit pays for the misses and lifts the account to the next level.

## Audit template (for an existing ad)
1. Map the ad into timed mini-blocks; tag each with the elements it serves.
2. Score all 17 elements: ✅ present / ⚠️ weak or late / ❌ missing.
3. Check *when* each sale happens. Sale 1 must land in the first 3 s; Sales 3–4 must exist (in the video, the end card,
   the headline or the primary text).
4. Fix in order of cost: re-cut existing footage → copy, headline, cards → new generated beats.
