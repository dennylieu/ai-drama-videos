# "Brazilify": the 4-sales, 17-element ad psychology

Adapted (own wording) from Derick Carneiro's article "how I write ads that make strangers buy" (X, Nov 2026).
Use it to brief and audit any cold-traffic ad: drama, UGC or VSL. Score every element present / weak / missing.

A cold ad does not sell the product. It makes 4 sales first, each with reasons the avatar ALREADY wants to hear.
The job is to find what they want to hear, how, and from whom; not to invent clever arguments.

## The 17 elements

**Sale 1: Content (watch me to the end)**
1. Format: why they lower their guard (looks like a show, a clip, news, not an ad).
2. Topic: why they care about this subject.
3. Concept: why they can't leave before the end (characters + narrative + topic in one logline).
4. Visual hook: what breaks autopilot in the first frame.
5. Copy hook: the first words that decide whether they keep watching.

**Sale 2: Hope (this time it can work)**
6. Identification: they feel understood.
7. Point of contact: what they already suspect causes the problem.
8. Mechanism of the problem: the missing reason that proves the suspicion right.
9. Mechanism of the function: the action needed to fix it, and why other solutions can't do it.
10. Mechanism of the solution: the one thing that does that action, with reasons.
11. Conditions: why they can't get it elsewhere or on their own (rare, hard, needs the right form).

**Sale 3: Value (this product, at this price)**
12. USP: why this product is the best or only place to get the mechanism under the right conditions.
13. Features: only those that raise the odds, make the dream tangible, cut the effort or shorten the time.
14. Anchoring: not solving it costs more (money, emotion, time) than the price.

**Sale 4: Impulse (now)**
15. Risk reversal: not buying is riskier than buying (guarantee framed against the real timeline).
16. Gain by buying now: bundles, free units, shipping, gifts.
17. Lose by waiting: price ends, stock runs out, no restock date.

## Process
1. **Premises:** brand, product, ingredients, attributes, price ladder, guarantee, what's gained now and lost later.
2. **Target:** market → segment (a cluster sharing a condition, identity or behaviour) → avatar (psychographics + identity:
   demographics, behaviour, beliefs, enemies, language). Copy markets and segments that already work for you or
   competitors (low risk); bet on concept and format (high leverage).
3. **Brief:** fill all 17 elements with what the avatar wants to hear. Mine competitors' winners for psychographics
   ("steal the research"); spend own research on behaviour: news, trends, shows, searches.
4. **Write:** split a proven reference ad into mini-blocks (hook, belief break, mechanism reveal, product, offer, close).
   The brief decides WHAT is said; the reference decides ORDER and RHYTHM.

Risk budget per batch: a few conservative ads, many moderate, some aggressive. Risk = number of new bets in one ad.
